A practical guide to your fitness data

How to use your fitness app

Learn how to log meals, understand calorie targets and deficits, review weekly insights, update your goals, and read your estimated weight graph. The estimates help you see patterns—they are not promises or medical advice.

1. A simple daily routine

Consistent, reasonably complete entries make the summaries more useful. A missed entry is not a failure—add what you can and continue.

1

Log meals as you go

Choose the meal and describe foods, portions, drinks, snacks, sauces, and cooking oil. Specific portions improve the AI estimate. Calories and macros are estimates, not exact measurements; edit a log if you know it needs correcting.

2

Check calories and protein

Compare the daily calories with your target and notice your protein total. Protein supports muscle repair and helps preserve muscle during weight change. Beans, lentils, eggs, dairy, tofu, fish, and meat are common sources; individual needs differ.

3

Save a daily check-in

Record weight, steps, exercise minutes, and activity notes in Daily Progress Check-In. Weigh under similar conditions when possible; ordinary day-to-day water and digestion changes can move the scale.

4

Review a date or a longer range

The dashboard date selector opens a particular day's entries. Trends and Weekly Insights let you choose a date range and look across multiple days rather than judging progress from one number.

2. Calories, deficit, and weight change

Calories are a measure of energy. Your maintenance estimate is the app's estimate of the daily energy intake that may keep weight broadly stable. Your target is calculated from your profile and goal timeline.

−

What is a calorie deficit?

A deficit means that, averaged over time, energy intake is below energy used. In this app, the planned daily deficit is approximately maintenance calories minus target calories. The dashboard's “Deficit Created” is a rough one-day comparison of maintenance and logged intake; it can be misleading if meals were not fully logged.

≈

How much deficit may affect weight?

As a rough illustration, an average 500 kcal/day gap adds up to 3,500 kcal/week. Using the simplified estimate of about 7,700 kcal per kg, that corresponds to around 0.45 kg/week on paper:

500 × 7 = 3,500 kcal/week; 3,500 ÷ 7,700 ≈ 0.45 kg

This is only a simplified energy conversion—not a guaranteed rate. Real weight change varies with water, adherence, body adaptation, activity, and estimation error.

Use a sustainable plan, not the largest possible deficit. The app estimates a plan from your details and applies a minimum-calorie safeguard when its formula would otherwise set an especially low target. A safe, suitable target depends on the individual; do not lower your target aggressively to force faster results. Ask a qualified health professional for personal advice, especially if you have a medical condition, are pregnant, or have a history of disordered eating.

3. Weekly Insights: what is in the report?

Open Weekly Insights from the dashboard. Set the From and To dates, then update the range. The report includes both endpoints and summarizes the tracking entries found within those dates.

↔

Daily breakdown

Each date row shows logged calories, itemized protein, steps, and weight when available. A zero-calorie day can mean no meals were recorded; it does not necessarily mean no food was eaten. Missing weight or activity entries are not interpreted as zero weight or inactivity.

AI

AI analysis and estimate

The coach reviews the daily summaries, your target, and profile context to describe patterns and offer suggestions. The report's estimated weight change uses the difference between the selected date range's total calorie target and logged intake, divided by about 7,700 kcal/kg. It is a rough estimate, not measured weight loss; missing or inaccurate food logs can distort it.

For the most useful report, log meals and check-ins consistently across the whole range. Compare the estimate with your logged weight trend and treat the AI feedback as guidance, not a diagnosis.

4. When and how to reanalyze your goals

Choose Reanalyze Goals when your current weight, target, activity, timeline, or circumstances have changed, or when you want the plan recalculated using updated information.

1

Update your details

Enter your current weight and desired target weight, activity level, timeline, and relevant context as accurately as possible. The app recalculates estimated maintenance calories, target calories, and the planned deficit from those details.

2

Review before accepting

Read the strategy summary and new milestones, including any note that your exact deadline may not be achievable within the app's calorie safeguards. Accept only if the plan looks appropriate; ask a qualified professional if unsure.

Reanalysis updates the goal plan and future estimate; it does not erase your meal or daily tracking history. Reanalysis History lets you revisit prior saved analyses.

5. How the estimated weight graph works

↗

Projected weekly milestones

During onboarding or reanalysis, the app calculates a calorie plan and asks the AI coach to generate weekly weight estimate points from the starting/current weight toward the estimated goal for the selected timeline. The graph connects those points to make the direction and schedule easier to view.

i

How to interpret it

The line is a planning illustration based on assumptions about calorie intake and a gradual trajectory. Actual weight does not usually fall in a perfectly straight line. Water changes, logging accuracy, activity, health, and individual response can all make real measurements differ. Use repeated weigh-ins and the Trends page to assess the longer-term pattern.

Predictions are estimates and are not a guarantee of results. The app's calorie math is simplified and cannot account for every medical or lifestyle factor.
